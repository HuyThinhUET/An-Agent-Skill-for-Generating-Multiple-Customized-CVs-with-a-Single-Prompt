# Web research for tailored CVs

Use web research only for a narrow unresolved question. A detailed JD is the primary targeting source and normally does not need web supplementation. Research may clarify a filename rule, a named recurring program, or a material gap in a sparse JD; it must never manufacture CV content or reverse-engineer a hiring process.

## Scope

Search only after reading the JD, current CV, personal information, and HR feedback. Skip it entirely when the JD is detailed and has no unresolved filename or program-context question. When needed, make the minimum number of targeted official-source queries, normally one or two:

- employer + exact role or program name, optionally with `CV filename` or `application instruction`;
- the employer's official careers, university, or program page when the JD is sparse or refers to a recurring named program.

Favor recent material. For programs run repeatedly, record the date or cycle and do not assume a historical review describes the current process.

When a filename rule is unresolved and an official recruitment page is readily available, look only for an explicit submission-filename requirement. Treat it as authoritative only when it is clear, current, and directly applicable to this role/program. If no direct official source is readily available, use the default naming rule rather than expanding the search.

## Source hierarchy and verification

Classify sources rather than trusting them uniformly:

1. **Primary:** the employer's careers page, official role description, program page, annual report, engineering blog, or official event material. Use these to establish current company facts and stated priorities.
2. **Corroborative:** reputable university, partner, or well-attributed media sources. Use these to confirm context when they agree with primary sources.
3. **Anecdotal:** applicant reviews, personal blogs, forums, and social posts. Do not seek these by default. They may at most suggest a question to verify through an official source; they cannot set CV content, a filename, or a hiring expectation.

A single anecdote must not change the CV. Let web research influence a material content or ordering decision only when it is consistent with the JD and candidate evidence, and—where it asserts a company/program fact—is confirmed by an independent source, preferably a primary source. Discard contradictions, unverifiable claims, paywalled snippets, vague reputation claims, and advice that pressures the candidate to exaggerate.

## Allowed and prohibited use

Allowed uses include selecting among already-supported projects, emphasizing an evidenced technology or domain, matching truthful terminology, and avoiding a stale or misleading framing. The CV may reflect the JD directly; it does not need web citations.

Never use web material to add a skill, metric, project feature, company affiliation, interview outcome, or achievement not supported by candidate materials. Do not copy review wording, claim knowledge of confidential selection criteria, or present advice from prior applicants as fact. Do not include personal candidate data in search queries or upload CV materials to third-party sites.

## Research record

Keep a private research note only if a web check was used and changed a decision: URL, source class, and the narrowly supported insight. If a web source sets the filename, record the exact rule and why it is authoritative. Do not place this note in the CV. In the final handoff, mention only material decisions the research changed and any meaningful uncertainty.

If web search is unavailable or produces no reliable insight, continue with the JD and local candidate evidence; the pipeline must not be blocked.
